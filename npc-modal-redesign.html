First Impression

Isabel cuts a figure of faded respectability—her dark cotton gown, though clean and carefully mended, bears the wear of many washings. Worry lines crease her tanned face, deepening when she speaks of her daughter's illness. Her cautious eyes dart between you and the shop entrance, as if expecting judgment.

Background

Widow of cloth merchant Diego Valdés. Struggling to maintain the family business alone while caring for her ailing daughter Teresa. Once part of Mexico City's prosperous middling class, now facing mounting debts and declining fortunes.

Social Standing

Criolla woman of middling class. Maintains respectability despite financial hardship. Known in the Plaza Mayor markets. Careful to observe proper decorum.

Appearance

Life History

1633: Born to a criolla family of modest means in Mexico City. Father worked as a notary's clerk.

1650: Married Diego Valdés, an ambitious cloth merchant, at age 17.

1661: Gave birth to daughter Teresa after several miscarriages. Family prospering.

1677: Husband died suddenly of fever. Business debts emerged. Lost the shop.

1680: Struggling to maintain business. Teresa increasingly ill. Mounting desperation.
